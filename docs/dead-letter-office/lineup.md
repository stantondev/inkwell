# Dead Letter Office lineup

The month-by-month plan for `/play`. A monthly research job (see
`drafting-a-week.md`, "The monthly job") reads this file, recommends the next
month's writers, drafts their weeks, and adds them here. Nothing goes live
until a person approves each week.

Why November looks like this: Lincoln's teaser promises a bundle "from across
the ocean" (Darwin); Helen Keller writes the plainest English of anyone, a good
easy week; Mark Twain is the funniest; Alcott falls on Thanksgiving week, with
her family letters; Dickens leads into December and Christmas.

## Used (never repeat a writer within a year)

Theodore Roosevelt, Abraham Lincoln, Charles Darwin, Helen Keller, Mark Twain,
Louisa May Alcott, Charles Dickens. Alexander Hamilton was drafted and set
aside (his 18th-century English was too hard for a daily puzzle).

## Ideas for later months

Each needs a public-domain edition with a plain text file. Check before
recommending.

- Robert Louis Stevenson: letters from the South Seas (Gutenberg #622, #637…)
- Jane Austen: letters to her sister Cassandra (Gutenberg #42078). Lively, but
  full of people nobody remembers; pick carefully.
- Vincent van Gogh: letters to his brother, in a pre-1931 English translation
- Emily Dickinson: letters (1894 edition), only the clearer ones
- Abigail and John Adams: letters between them (spelling may be too old)
- Benjamin Franklin: letters to his family
- John Keats: letters (1895 edition)
- Florence Nightingale, Frederick Douglass, Clara Barton: check what's online
- Seasonal ideas: a Christmas week of letters about Christmas from several
  writers is not allowed (one writer a week), but a writer famous for Christmas
  (Dickens) or for winter journeys (Scott's Antarctic letters, published 1913)
  fits.
