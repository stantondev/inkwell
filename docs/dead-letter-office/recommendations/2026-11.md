## 26 Oct · Abraham Lincoln (`lincoln.ts`)

Mary Owens, 1837 · John D. Johnston, 1848 · Grace Bedell, 1860 · Horace Greeley, 1862 · Fanny McCullough, 1862

- Tue: the edition dates the Johnston letter 2 Jan 1851; the standard Collected Works dates it 24 Dec 1848 (used, with a note).
- Mon: Mary Owens "about a year older", Elizabeth Abell "first suggested the match", and her later quote "deficient in those little links…".
- Mon: he rode into Springfield on a borrowed horse, with everything in two saddlebags.
- Tue: he did not visit his dying father in January 1851; visited his stepmother in January 1861.
- Wed: on the Westfield platform he said he'd grown the whiskers for her, and kissed her; Grace married George Billings, lived in Delphos, Kansas, died 1936.
- Fri: William McCullough had lost an arm, was nearly blind in one eye, and enlisted at 49.
- Fri: Collected Works reads "bitterest agony" where the edition has "bittered" (noted).

## 2 Nov · Charles Darwin (`darwin.ts`)

His father, 1831 · Joseph Hooker, 1844 · Charles Lyell, 1858 · Asa Gray, 1860 · Sarah Haliburton, 1872

- Tue: Hooker at "Kew, near London" in Jan 1844; his father William directed Kew then. The Hooker letter is an extract, with no greeting or signature.
- Fri: Mrs. Haliburton's address given only as "near London" (possibly Isleworth).
- Mon: "the car" in the 1831 letter is left unexplained.
- Wed: historians argue about exactly when Wallace's essay arrived (noted).
- Thu: Gray as a devout Christian who thought variation might be guided; working to get American publishers to pay the author.
- Cards: Huxley "praised the book in The Times" (anonymously); Henrietta "married in 1871, living in London"; Hooker "four years in the Antarctic with James Ross".
- Timeline: his last book on earthworms (1881); buried in Westminster Abbey near Newton; mother died when he was eight.

## 9 Nov · Helen Keller (`keller.ts`)

Alexander Graham Bell, 1887 · Sarah Fuller, 1890 · Edward H. Clement, 1892 · her sister Mildred, 1899 · the Academic Board of Radcliffe, 1900

- Her father Arthur, a former Confederate captain who edited the town paper.
- Anne Sullivan was twenty when she arrived and nearly blind herself; the water pump came "a month later" (April 1887).
- Mrs. Lamson had taught at Perkins; Laura Bridgman taught there half a century before.
- Clement as editor of the Boston Evening Transcript.
- Thu: Mildred at home in Tuscumbia; the 18 Nov 1899 Harvard–Yale game ended 0–0; Roosevelt "then governor of New York". Two sentences where she says "we could hear" the crowd were cut.
- Fri: Radcliffe "took weeks" and said yes; cum laude in 1904 as the first deaf and blind person to earn a bachelor's degree; Ladies' Home Journal serialized her story in 1902.
- Later life: American Foundation for the Blind from the 1920s; campaigning for votes for women; Anne Sullivan died 1936; Helen died 1 June 1968 at 87.
- Merton Keith as her tutor in Latin and Greek.

## 16 Nov · Mark Twain (`twain.ts`)

his sister Pamela, 1853 · "Aunt Betsey" Smith, 1859 · the Twichells, 1870 · his daughter Susy, 1892 · Helen Keller, 1903

- Tue: the year is Paine's "[probably 1859]" (noted); Bixby's $500 fee from 1857, licence April 1859; brother Henry died the summer before.
- Mon: Pamela eight years older, married to William Moffett; by 1854 he was back in Orion's print shop in Iowa.
- Wed: Jervis Langdon gave the Buffalo house as a wedding present; baby Langdon died of diphtheria in June 1872.
- Thu: Susy began her biography of him at thirteen and died of meningitis in Hartford in August 1896, aged 24. "Plug" read as the lens cap.
- Fri: Henry Rogers helped pay for Helen Keller's schooling; she was at Radcliffe then (`deliverTo` just says Massachusetts).
- His signatures "Sam", "Clemens" and "Mark" are all smudged; "Innocents Abroad" is smudged too. He keeps one "Oh, dam—".

## 23 Nov · Louisa May Alcott (`alcott.ts`, Thanksgiving week)

her mother, Christmas 1854 · her sister Anna, 1863 · Thomas Niles, 1870 · her father, Thanksgiving 1875 · Mrs. Bond, 1888

- Wed: the edition gives no place; Vevey is inferred from her letters around it (noted). Silling's as a school in Vevey is inferred.
- Tue and Fri: `deliverTo` given as "near Boston"; Thu: her father at home in Concord in Nov 1875.
- Mrs. Bond described only as "an old friend of the family".
- Tue: "L. C. on the pincushion" read as the expected baby girl's initials; her 1863 fever called typhoid pneumonia.
- Thu: she spent Thanksgiving 1875 dining at Anne Lynch Botta's (her reading of the letter).
- Dates: mother died Nov 1877; May died 1879, weeks after Lulu was born; father's stroke 1882; Concord School of Philosophy 1879; Orchard House 1858.
- "Never ill before that winter, and never well after" paraphrases a line the edition quotes.

## 30 Nov · Charles Dickens (`dickens.ts`)

Hastings Hughes (a boy), 1838 · Thomas Mitton, 1842 · Elizabeth Gaskell, 1850 · Clarkson Stanfield, 1854 · his son Plorn, 1868

- Mon: Squeers, Nicholas, Smike and Fanny Squeers are smudged as the writer's own until he's named (they'd give him away on day one). `deliverTo` is just "England".
- Mon: his midwinter trip to the Yorkshire schools in 1838; the schools closing soon after.
- Tue: copyright speeches at dinners and the papers turning on him; not back in America for 25 years.
- Wed: only his name appeared on Household Words pages; "Lizzie Leigh" in the first number; quarrels over North and South.
- Thu: he gave the Sherborne reading the week before Christmas 1854; Stanfield in Hampstead.
- Fri: written September 1868, place not given; his sons' whereabouts (Walter died in India 1863, Alfred in Australia from 1865); Plorn later sat in the New South Wales parliament and died in 1902.
- Edition slips kept with notes: "beat fitted" (best), "Rev. Thomas Barham" (Richard Harris Barham).
